Data and precision
  • Planets: JPL approximate positions (Standish). Table 1 for 1800–2050 (≤ 10′), Tables 2a+2b for 3000 BC–AD 3000 (≤ 34′).
  • Moon: Astronomical Almanac series, about ±0.5°. Its distance from Earth is drawn enlarged.
  • Moons of Mars, Jupiter, Saturn: JPL mean elements. Orbital phase only; Titan's starting phase is approximate.
  • Voyager 1: JPL Horizons trajectory 1977–2030, straight-line extrapolation after.
  • Surfaces: NASA Blue Marble Next Generation (Earth); NASA 3D Resources maps for Mars, Venus (Magellan radar under a cloud veil), Jupiter, Saturn, Neptune, the Galilean moons, Titan, Phobos and Deimos. Sky: NASA Tycho star map. Earth turns by true sidereal time.
  • Spin axes: IAU WGCCRE 2015 poles. Physical data: NASA fact sheet.
  • Heliosphere: Voyager 1 crossings (94 and 121.6 AU), IBEX inflow direction (Swaczyna et al. 2022). Galaxy: Hipparcos frame, Sun at 251.9 km/s (Reid & Brunthaler 2020, Schönrich et al. 2010).
  • Galactic drift speed on screen is illustrative unless you choose the true ratio.
  • Galaxies: positions from galactic coordinates; distances from McConnachie 2012, Pietrzyński 2019 (LMC), Graczyk 2020 (SMC), Li 2021 (M31), Mei 2007 (Virgo), Blakeslee 2009 (Fornax), redshift with H₀ = 70 beyond 40 Mpc. Milky Way arms are a log-spiral model. Laniakea outline is schematic (Tully 2014).

Positions are computed live in your browser from published scientific data. Nothing is animated by hand.

  • Planets use JPL's Keplerian elements (Standish): within 10′ for 1800–2050 and within 34′ for 3000 BC–AD 3000.
  • Voyager 1 follows JPL Horizons. Tested: it reaches 24.0 light-hours from Earth on 18 Nov 2026, the date NASA announced.
  • Earth turns by true sidereal time, so the side facing the Sun matches the real time of day.
  • The Moon uses the Astronomical Almanac series (±0.5°). Planetary moons use JPL mean elements for their phase.
  • Galaxies and clusters sit at catalogued sky positions and measured distances. Beyond 40 Mpc, distances come from redshift with H₀ = 70.

What is approximate

  • Planet sizes are enlarged, and distances can be shown compressed. Directions are always exact.
  • The Milky Way's arms are a log-spiral model. Nobody has mapped its far side.
  • Galaxies and nebulae are rendered from their type, size and orientation, not from photographs. Their rotation is sped up about 30 trillion times.
  • Laniakea's outline is schematic.
  • Earth: NASA Blue Marble Next Generation, via NASA WorldWind.
  • Mars, Venus (Magellan radar), Jupiter, Saturn, Neptune, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Titan, Phobos, Deimos and the Tycho star map: NASA 3D Resources, free and without copyright.
  • Webb and Hubble images of the Cosmic Cliffs, Southern Ring Nebula, Tarantula Nebula, Stephan's Quintet, Cartwheel Galaxy and Webb's First Deep Field: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI.
  • Ephemerides: JPL Solar System Dynamics and JPL Horizons. Physical data: NASA NSSDCA. Spin axes: IAU WGCCRE 2015.
  • Galaxy and heliosphere measurements: GRAVITY 2021, Reid & Brunthaler 2020, Schönrich et al. 2010, Bennett & Bovy 2019, McConnachie 2012, Pietrzyński et al. 2019, Graczyk et al. 2020, Li et al. 2021, Mei et al. 2007, Blakeslee et al. 2009, Tully et al. 2014, Swaczyna et al. 2022 (IBEX).
  • Built with three.js. Type: Syne, Manrope and JetBrains Mono.
